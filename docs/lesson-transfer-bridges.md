# Related observation bridges

The existing lessons already offer diagrams, artwork and concrete offline observations. This change only makes the next lesson's comparative purpose visible: what carries across and what changes. Sixteen original directed bridge prompts replace title-only buttons; existing lesson IDs, choices, storage and navigation safeguards are unchanged. There is no required sequence or completion reward.

Evidence reviewed 2026-11-12:
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2018), *How People Learn II*, chapter 3, p.55: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/5 . The research synthesis distinguishes memory practice from transfer-oriented comparison of multiple instances and reflection on why a phenomenon occurs. This motivates explicit comparative questions, not a promise of learning gains.
- National Research Council (2000), *How People Learn*, chapter 3: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6 . Remembering learned material does not establish successful use in another context. Do not treat completed records as measured transfer.

These sources do not validate Room Rubato's prompts, app retention, aesthetic skill gains, or cross-domain equivalence of preferences. Music-to-visual bridges explicitly preserve that distinction. Prompts are Room Rubato editorial work, not copied teaching routines or institutional endorsements. No personal responses were used in research and no analytics were added.

Synthetic verification: each existing related edge appears exactly once, targets a real lesson and offers an explicit question; each destination remains freely selectable; the integrated notebook transition creates no record or answer. Existing interrupted-edit and completed-record tests remain applicable.
