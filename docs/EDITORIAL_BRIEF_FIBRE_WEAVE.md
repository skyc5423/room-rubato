# Editorial trial: fibre is not weave

Status: feasibility-reviewed brief, not an implemented or published lesson. Reviewed 2026-11-16. Applies EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md to one real candidate; no new production route, collection or publishing cadence.

## Gap and bounded outcome

Working title: **실크와 새틴은 같은 종류의 말일까요?** Proposed entry: the fashion section of /explore, next to the existing contrast bridge, only after implementation acceptance. The current bridge explicitly does not reproduce fabric or fit. Adding another colour contrast would not resolve that conceptual gap.

One outcome: distinguish a fibre/material word from a weave/structure word. Do not claim that identifying either lets someone judge quality, comfort, value or suitability from a screen. Narrow the earlier “material/surface” idea rather than simulate realistic shine.

## Verified primary evidence and asset decision

- Canadian Conservation Institute, [Caring for textiles and costumes](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/textiles-costumes.html), “Natural fibres” and “Woven textiles”, read 2026-11-16: silk is a fibre; woven fabric interlaces warp and weft; plain, twill and satin are structures. This supports the category distinction, not a prediction of the finished garment. Figures 7–9 expressly credit Royal Ontario Museum with permission. **Do not copy their diagrams or photographs.**
- US National Park Service, [Weaving Patterns](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/weaving-patterns.htm), “Plain Weave” and “Satin”, read 2026-11-16: plain weave alternates crossings; satin here describes a structure, not a single fibre. The article itself notes variable satin/sateen usage. Scope the text to **새틴 조직** rather than claim universal retail naming rules. No images or wording are imported.
- Neither source tests learning efficacy or retention for this service. This is an independently authored editorial explanation, not a research-proven intervention or institutional endorsement.
- User-named aemiga_ originals remain inaccessible in prior review. No creator-specific teaching method, quotation, screenshot or imitation is attributed to that account. MFA CAMEO pages returned 403 and are not treated as verified evidence.

## Concrete draft and storyboard

1. Open with two original text cards, “실크” and “새틴 조직”, followed by “옷감 이름처럼 보이지만, 서로 다른 것을 설명해요.” This asks for observation, not a scored quiz.
2. Reveal: “실크는 섬유를, 새틴 조직은 실이 교차하는 방식을 가리켜요.” Provide the primary links adjacent to the explanation.
3. Optional original plain-weave schematic: a small grid with clearly labelled vertical/horizontal yarns and alternating over/under crossings. Caption: “실이 교차하는 순서만 보여주는 도식입니다. 실제 원단의 촉감이나 광택을 재현하지 않아요.” No copied figure geometry, simulated folds, shader or stock fabric photograph.
4. Keep colour, yarn width, cell size and viewing angle fixed. If a second weave is later added, separately validate its repeat/crossing rule before calling it satin; **do not** label a generic checkerboard satin. The current brief does not need a second weave to teach the category distinction.
5. Close: “다음에 옷 설명을 볼 때, 무엇으로 만들었는지와 어떻게 짰는지를 나눠 읽어보세요.” Optional return action uses a new real-world example, not a daily streak or claim of fresh content. No purchase or fabric handling required.
